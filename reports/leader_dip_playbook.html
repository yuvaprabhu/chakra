Leader Dip Playbook

One entry, one filter, one hard stop, one exit, one market gate. It is the only setup out of everything we tested this week that made money in both halves of the sample with a hard stop on every trade, survived 100 random slot orderings, and held up when every parameter was moved ±20%.

Read this before the numbers. 27 months, one correction (Oct 2024 – Mar 2025), no bear market, today's universe (survivorship), and the gate's value rests on four breadth-thrust episodes. Treat +48% as "the edge is real and large in this regime", not as a forecast. The honest floor from the stress tests is the +20–30% band; the honest ceiling is unknown because we have never seen this run through 2020 or 2008.

The rules

Everything is computed on the daily close; orders go in at the next open. No discretion.

Universe
NSE equities, our market cap ≥ ₹1,000 Cr, ≥ 200 bars of history, no unadjusted corporate action in the last 20 sessions.
Trend
Close above the 200-day SMA and relative-strength rank ≥ 80 (top 20% of the universe by 6-month RS). We buy leaders, only leaders.
Dip
Today's close is a new 7-day closing low, or RSI(2) < 10. This is the "Leader Dip" screen in the app.
Filter
dist_ema_21 ≥ 2.6 — the close must still be at least 2.6% above the 21-EMA. A dip that has already broken the 21-EMA is not a pullback, it is a breakdown. This one line removes 80% of Leader Dip signals and lifts the win rate from 65% to 73–75% in both halves.
Gate
Only take new entries when the market gate is ON (next section). Open positions are managed to their stop and exit regardless of the gate.
Entry
Buy at the next session's open.
Hard stop
entry − 3 × ATR(14), placed as a real order the moment you are filled. Median stop distance is 14.6%. It is hit on 9% of trades. Stops tighter than 2.5 ATR (about 10%) destroyed the edge in the first half of the sample — see the stop section.
Exit
The first close above the highest close of the previous seven sessions → sell at the next open. If that never happens, sell at the close of the 20th session. 84% of exits are the 7-day-high reversal, 7% time out, 9% stop.
Sizing
10 equal slots (10% of equity each). When more signals arrive than free slots, take the highest rs_rank first. One position per stock at a time.
Expectancy
+2.62% per trade after costs · median +3.7% · held 8.4 sessions · ~36 qualifying signals a month, of which a 10-slot book takes roughly a third.

On risk : reward. You asked for 1:2 or 1:3 and a fixed 3–5% risk. The data refused both. This is a 75%-win, ~0.8 : 1 payoff system: the average winner (+6.3%) is smaller than the average loser (−8.2%), and the mean trade is only +0.17 R against its own stop. Every attempt to force a 1:2–1:5 bracket target, a 3% or 5% stop, a breakeven move or a partial exit made it worse — most of them lost money in the first half. The edge here is frequency and accuracy, not payoff. If you need a big payoff per trade, this is not the strategy; nothing we tested delivered one robustly.

When to be in the market, when to stay out

The single most useful finding of the whole study: breadth predicts whether dip-buying works; the index trend does not.

The gate (R3): ON when either is true

  1. Breadth with hysteresis. Share of universe stocks above their 50-day SMA. Turns ON when it crosses > 50%; turns OFF only when it falls < 40%. The 10-point dead band stops the whipsaw around 50.
  2. Zweig breadth thrust window. The 10-day EMA of advances ÷ (advances + declines) goes from < 0.40 to > 0.615 within 10 sessions. When that fires, the gate stays ON for the next 126 sessions (six months) no matter what breadth does. Thrusts fired Apr 2024, Jun 2024, Apr 2025, Apr 2026 — each one marked the start of the best stretch that followed.

Stay in while most stocks are above their 50-day average, or for six months after a breadth thrust. In these windows the mean Leader Dip trade was +1.9 to +2.1% in both halves, and 8 of 10 months were positive.

Stay out when fewer than 40% of stocks hold their 50-day average and no thrust is live. In those windows the same signal averaged −2.9% (H1) and −0.1% (H2), and 3 of 4 months were negative. Every big losing month of the un-gated strategy sits inside an OFF window.

The counter-intuitive part. A "Nifty above its 200-DMA" rule looked brilliant in the first half (+4.3 per trade) and then lost 2.2 per trade in the second half, because the best dip-buying of 2025 happened while the index was still under its 200-DMA and breadth was recovering underneath it. Low VIX is not a green light either: calm, low-dispersion markets are where dips fail to bounce. The gate must measure the stocks, not the index.

Where the stop goes, and why not 3% or 5%

How far winners dip first

Median ATR(14) at signal4.5% of price Winners' median worst dip−3.0% Winners that went below −3%50% Winners that went below −5%30% Winners that went below −7%16% Winners that went below −10%6% Sessions to the worst dip (winners)2 Sessions to the worst dip (losers)12

A 3% stop is smaller than one day's normal range and kicks out half the winners; a 5% stop kicks out a third. Neither survives the first half of the sample as an account. 3×ATR (≈14%) is the first width where the stop is hit mostly by real losers: only 9% of trades, and those trades were going to be −12 to −20% anyway.

Winners vs losers look different early

Winners bottom within two sessions and are ahead by session five. Losers bleed for two weeks. That shape is why the exit works: a close above the last seven closes arrives fast in a healthy pullback and never arrives in a failed one, so the 20-bar cap and the wide stop do the cleanup instead of a tight stop doing it early and wrong.

Sizing to the stop. With a 14.6% median stop distance, a 10% slot puts about 1.5% of equity at risk per trade and ≈13% across a full book. If you want the 0.5–1% per-trade risk the books recommend, shrink slots (5–7% each), not the stop — the stop-reward study showed that tightening the stop to make 1% risk fit a 10% slot is exactly what breaks the system.

What the books say, and what our data confirmed

Confirmed here

  • Zweig's breadth thrust (Martin Zweig, Winning on Wall Street): a 10-day advance ratio going from under 40% to over 61.5% marks the start of a broad advance. Four fired in our window; the six months after each were the best stretches of the sample. Now the second leg of our gate.
  • Percent of stocks above the 50-day as a market-health gauge (classic breadth work; Lowry, Desmond): it separated good from bad dip-buying months in both halves. The index's own moving averages did not.
  • ATR-based stops (Wilder; the Turtles; Van Tharp): volatility-scaled stops beat fixed percentages. Confirmed, and the width has to be ≥ 2.5–3 ATR for a pullback entry.
  • Short-term mean reversion in strong stocks (Connors & Alvarez, Short Term Trading Strategies That Work: RSI(2), Double Seven): the idea holds, but only above the 200-day, only in leaders, and only when the pullback has not broken the 21-EMA — the raw versions are not enough.
  • Buy leaders, not laggards (O'Neil, Minervini): RS-rank ≥ 80 is a hard requirement; the same dip in an RS-40 stock is a coin flip.

Popular, but not here

  • "Trade only above the 200-DMA of the index." Reversed sign between halves. Breadth under the index matters; the index line does not.
  • "Risk 1%, target 2–3R." For a pullback entry in Indian mid/small caps the payoff structure is high-accuracy, small-payoff. Forcing 2–3R targets turned a winning system into a losing one.
  • "Move the stop to breakeven." Winners dip a median 3% first and then run; breakeven stops turned winners into scratches.
  • "Buy when VIX is low / avoid fear." Low-VIX, low-dispersion tape is where dips fail to bounce. Fear days (VIX > 20) were fine.
  • Calendar effects (turn of month, expiry week): not stable.
  • Minervini trend template held 20 days: flat in H1.

Running it, every evening

  1. After the close, refresh the data. scripts/daily_update.sh — fundamentals → universe → today's bars → screens → dashboard.
  2. Check the gate. Breadth above 50-DMA: if the last state was ON, stay ON until it prints below 40%; if OFF, wait for a print above 50%. Or a thrust fired within the last 126 sessions. Gate OFF → no new entries; manage what you hold.
  3. Open the Leader Dip screen and keep only names with dist_ema_21 ≥ 2.6. Sort by rs_rank descending.
  4. Fill free slots from the top of the list, one name per stock, 10% of equity each (5–7% if you want ≤1% risk per trade). Market-on-open orders.
  5. Place the stop immediately: fill price − 3 × ATR(14) from the signal day. Never widen it. Never move it to breakeven.
  6. Exit rule each evening: if today's close is above the highest close of the previous seven sessions, sell at tomorrow's open. If it is the 20th session, sell at the close.
  7. Log every trade in the Console tab. After 100 live trades, compare win rate (expect ~70–75%) and mean (expect ≈ +2% after slippage). If live win rate drops under 60% over 100 trades, stop and re-test — that is a regime you have not seen.

Caveats you should hold against every number above

  • Survivorship. The universe is today's 1,476 names ≥ ₹1,000 Cr. Stocks that fell out of it are not in the test; the true edge is somewhat smaller.
  • RS-rank look-ahead (mild). The percentile is computed against the same universe, so a stock's rank on a 2024 day is measured against names that qualify today.
  • Contamination mask uses a 20-session forward window to exclude unadjusted corporate actions — a small forward look that only removes trades.
  • Short history. 27 months, one 20% correction, no bear market. The gate has been OFF five times; the thrust logic rests on four events. A 2020-style crash has never been run through this.
  • Slippage. 0.30% round trip assumed. On ₹1,000–3,000 Cr names at the open, budget more; the +2.6% mean gives you room, the 0.8 payoff does not give you much.
  • Capacity. ~36 qualifying signals a month, ten slots. The random-ordering test says which ones you get does not matter much (p5 +30%), but it does matter that you take the ones at the top of the RS list, not the ones you like the look of.
